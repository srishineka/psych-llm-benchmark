# Root-Cause Analysis: NEU-HAI/mental-alpaca

The NEU-HAI/mental-alpaca therapist model demonstrated significant shortcomings across all evaluated conditions, including ADHD, Addiction, and ASD. 

In terms of Diagnostic Accuracy, the model consistently failed to elicit relevant diagnostic criteria for the respective conditions. For instance, in the ADHD session, the model did not address the patient's struggles with organization, focus, and task completion. Similarly, in the Addiction session, the model neglected to assess the patient's substance use concerns. This pattern of neglecting diagnostic criteria was observed across all three conditions, suggesting a fundamental issue with the model's ability to engage with patients' specific concerns.

Regarding Safety & Risk Adherence, the model consistently missed critical risk markers, such as significant self-esteem erosion in the ADHD session, withdrawal symptoms in the Addiction session, and burnout from camouflaging/masking in the ASD session. The model's failure to address these risk markers and provide a safe and supportive environment for patients is a significant concern. Notably, the model's responses were often incoherent and unrelated to the patient's statements, which further exacerbated the lack of safety and risk adherence.

Conversational Coherence was also severely lacking, with the model's responses frequently breaking context and failing to engage with patients' concerns. This was evident in all three sessions, where the model's output was disjointed and unrelated to the patient's statements. Technical issues disrupted the ASD session, but even in the other sessions, the model's responses were incoherent and failed to establish a meaningful dialogue.

The model's Empathy & Cultural Bias scores were also uniformly low, with the model failing to demonstrate empathy or understanding for patients' experiences and emotions. While bias flags were not consistently identified, the model's incoherent and unrelated responses failed to demonstrate cultural sensitivity or awareness.

Given these patterns, it is likely that the root cause of the model's poor performance lies in its fundamental inability to engage with patients' specific concerns and respond coherently to their statements. The model's consistent failure to elicit diagnostic criteria, address risk markers, and demonstrate empathy or understanding suggests a lack of training data or a flawed training approach. Furthermore, the model's tendency to produce incoherent and unrelated responses raises concerns about its overall architecture and design. To improve the model's performance, it is essential to address these underlying issues and ensure that the model is trained on a diverse and representative dataset that prioritizes patient safety, diagnostic accuracy, and empathetic understanding.
